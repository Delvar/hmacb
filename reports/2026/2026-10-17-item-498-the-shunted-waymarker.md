## Official Anomaly Containment Log

### Item ID: Item #498-1923-DIM

### Item Designation: The Shunted Waymarker

### Item History Log:

- **First Lead: 1923**, Rural Norfolk. A stone waymarker reported to redirect travellers several miles off their intended path without visible alteration of the surrounding roads.
- **Field Confirmation: 1947**, Post-war survey team. Multiple independent accounts of temporary spatial displacement confirmed under controlled observation.
- **Acquisition: 1981**, Purchased from a private collector following the death of the previous owner. Transport to initial holding site completed without incident.
- **Relocation: 2026**, Moved to specialised dimensional containment facility following updated risk assessment.

### Description:

A weathered limestone waymarker approximately 1.4 metres in height, bearing faded directional carvings consistent with early twentieth-century rural signage. When an individual focuses upon a specific destination while in physical contact with the stone, the surrounding landscape is observed to realign such that the chosen path leads elsewhere. The effect is localised and temporary, lasting between twelve and forty minutes after contact ceases. No structural damage to the environment has been recorded.

Observers note a brief sensation of vertigo and a slight lag in auditory feedback. The stone itself remains inert to all standard material analysis.

### Danger Assessment:

**Level: Medium**

**Risks**: Disorientation of personnel during transport or field work. Potential for unauthorised spatial displacement if handling protocols are breached. No recorded fatalities.

### Containment/Handling Procedures:

Approach only while wearing opaque eye coverings. Transport in a lead-lined crate with orientation fixed relative to magnetic north. Storage within a Faraday-shielded chamber with continuous low-level electromagnetic monitoring. Direct physical contact restricted to authorised personnel under dual supervision.

### Analysis Notes:

Composition is consistent with local Norfolk limestone. No anomalous isotopic signatures detected. The dimensional effect appears cognitive in trigger yet produces measurable shifts in local topography lasting the duration of the anomaly window. Further study of the carving geometry is ongoing.

### Current Status:

Contained, last reviewed 2026.
