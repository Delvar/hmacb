## Official Anomaly Containment Log

### Item ID: Item #474-2025-MAG

### Item Designation: The Cindered Quill

### Item History Log:

- **First Lead: 1889**, Edinburgh. A writing quill observed to produce text that smouldered faintly after the ink had dried.
- **Investigation: 1912**, Multiple literary societies reported spontaneous combustion of manuscripts written with the instrument, limited to the inked portions only.
- **Acquisition: 2024**, Recovered from a private collection in York. Transport completed under standard fire-suppression protocols.
- **Review: 2025**, Reassessment confirmed no escalation in effect over the preceding century.

### Description:

A goose-feather quill of conventional manufacture, the shaft blackened as if partially burnt yet remaining structurally sound. When used with ordinary ink the written characters emit a low thermal signature for approximately three minutes after application. The paper itself is unaffected beyond minor discolouration at the edges of each stroke. The effect ceases entirely once the ink has fully dried.

No residual heat remains after the brief period. The quill produces no anomalous readings when not in use.

### Danger Assessment:

**Level: Low**

**Risks**: Minor thermal output during active writing. Negligible risk of ignition under normal conditions. No recorded injuries.

### Containment/Handling Procedures:

Store in a standard fire-resistant document box. Use only under observation when active writing is required for study. No special transport requirements beyond ordinary care.

### Analysis Notes:

Material composition matches period goose quill. The thermal effect is confined to the ink-paper interface and dissipates rapidly. Hypothesis of residual alchemical treatment remains unconfirmed. Effect stable across repeated trials.

### Current Status:

Contained, last reviewed 2025.
